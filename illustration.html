Published Works

The Genetic and Morphological Basis of Local Adaptation to Elevational Extremes in an Alpine Finch

Alpine systems are experiencing rapid environmental change, making it increasingly important to understand local adaptation for conservation, particularly in high-elevation species like the Sierra Nevada Gray-crowned Rosy-Finch. By integrating morphological and genomic analyses, this study found that temperature, food availability, and alpine landscape features impose complex and sometimes conflicting selective pressures on traits linked to thermoregulation and altitude adaptation. As first author on this paper, I drew all illustrations and created all figures.

A widespread gap in U.S. Endangered Species Act implementation: Risk of genetic erosion within populations

We apply indicators of intraspecific genetic diversity as adopted by the Convention on Biological Diversity to 161 species assessed for classification under the U.S. Endangered Species Act (ESA). In addition to contributions to data analysis and writing, I illustrated all animals and developed all figures for this paper.
